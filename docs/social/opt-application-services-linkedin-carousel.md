## Outside LinkedIn post caption

Before an F-1 or OPT student pays an “apply-for-you” service, there is one question to ask:

**Who is really submitting the application?**

Meet Arjun, a fictional OPT student who joins an application service because his unemployment clock is running. He shares his resume and work-authorization details. A specialist submits applications for him. Then a form asks about future sponsorship.

If that answer is wrong, the recruiter will still treat it as Arjun’s answer.

This is not a claim that ApplyRyt, NextGenApply, or Hustle Hive is illegal, unsafe, or guaranteed to cause an account restriction. It is a reminder to check the workflow before you pay:

• Who presses Submit?
• Can you approve the exact resume and screening answers first?
• Does anyone need your LinkedIn, Indeed, or email password?
• Where are applications submitted from?
• Can you retrieve the exact application after submission?

LinkedIn and Indeed publish restrictions on certain automated activity. Greenhouse documents device, IP, email, and phone signals for fraud assessment. None of those sources gives us a reliable auto-apply ban rate or a universal application-count threshold.

Use technology to save time. Keep control of what employers receive under your name.
